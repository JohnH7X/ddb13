Servitudes

The Servitude Question — November, 2026

Tonight, you will be asked to approve two servitudes relating to the project.

01

Infrastructure Servitude

Under the current arrangement, the co-owners remain responsible for the costs associated with the repair and maintenance of the aqueduct, sewer system, and drainage infrastructure located beneath the street, including in the event of a malfunction or pipe failure.

The proposed infrastructure servitude would transfer responsibility for the repair and maintenance of this infrastructure to the City of Laval, thereby removing this potential financial liability from the co-owners.

02

Reduced Right-of-Passage Servitude

The second servitude concerns a substantially reduced pedestrian right of passage. The proposed route goes through Panorama, where the City already has a servitude.

Rather than creating a passage through the project, it provides only a short pedestrian connection between the existing walking path on Étienne-Lavoie and the waterfront path that continues through Panorama. The City requires a small connection to the path.

Benefits

Infrastructure Servitude

Once this servitude is granted, the syndicate of co-ownership will no longer be financially responsible for repairing the underground water, drainage, and sewer systems if they are damaged or fail.

Right of Passage

Residents will benefit from a multi-use pathway extending from the existing path on Étienne-Lavoie to the waterfront.

Plans for Approval

The attached plans provide:

  • a visual representation showing the location and extent of the two servitudes; and
  • the corresponding legal and technical plan defining the servitudes for approval.

The objective tonight is straightforward: transfer the liability for municipal infrastructure to the City of Laval and grant a right of passage for the continuation of the pedestrian walkway from Étienne-Lavoie.

Questions & Answers

Aquablu Servitudes Q&A

QuestionWhy are the co-owners being asked to approve these servitudes?

AnswerThe purpose is to clarify responsibilities and reduce the burden and uncertainty currently carried by the co-owners. The infrastructure servitude is intended to transfer responsibility for the repair and maintenance of the aqueduct, sewer, and drainage infrastructure beneath the street to the City of Laval.

QuestionDoes the right-of-passage servitude give the public broad access through Aquablu?

AnswerNo. The proposed right of passage has been substantially reduced and is confined to one side of the project, adjacent to the Panorama seniors' residence. It is essentially a short connection between the existing walking path on Étienne-Lavoie and the waterfront path leading toward the seniors' residence.

QuestionWill this create a major new pathway through the development?

AnswerNo. The intention is to create a limited, clearly defined connection, not a general right of passage throughout the Aquablu property.

QuestionWhat is the principal benefit to the co-owners?

AnswerThe most significant benefit is the infrastructure arrangement. Rather than leaving the co-owners exposed to potentially substantial costs if the underground municipal infrastructure fails, the arrangement is intended to place responsibility for that infrastructure with the City of Laval.

QuestionWill this extension to the walking path cost Aquablu owners anything?

AnswerNo. All costs associated with extending the path will be covered by the City of Laval.

QuestionCan co-owners see exactly where the servitudes are located?

AnswerYes. The accompanying plans provide both a visual representation and the technical legal description of the areas affected by the servitudes.
